How to read the curve

The pale curve shows the share of marine animal genera (groups of related species) that vanished in each geological stage. It uses genera because fossils of whole groups survive better than fossils of single species. The values are approximate and redrawn after Rohde & Muller (2005), who used Sepkoski's fossil compendium.

Why the scale changes

Most of the story happens in the last half-billion years, and the human part happens in the last few centuries. Each dashed line on the fresco marks a zoom. The label beside it says how many years one pixel stands for in that section.

What we can't count

Before about 540 million years ago, life was mostly microscopic and left few fossils, so nobody can put a percentage on those crises. The same goes for the ice age and recorded history, where we know individual species instead of fossil percentages. Those sections show events without a curve.
